Our concept: an exoskeleton for elderly people with an added airbag to prevent broken bones. Similar products exist in some countries but are usually expensive and not adapted to Thailand's climate (humidity etc.). The exoskeleton reduces the impact of a fall on the joints and can help the person get up again, because lying on the floor for hours makes the damage much bigger. Callouts on the render: perforated humidity-adapted ventilation panels, central control unit with fall-detection sensors, actuated hip and knee joints with integrated airbags, sealed power supply and gas canister, assist-to-stand actuation.

Product description

Meet SafeStep.

A lightweight robotic exoskeleton that improves mobility and safety for elderly people in Thailand, built on three technologies.

1

AI motion prediction

IMU, joint and pressure sensors predict movement intent and adapt the assistance.

2

Robotic assist + safety clutch

BLDC motors and planetary gearboxes help the hips; a torque-limiting clutch stops excess force.

3

Fall detection + airbag

Detects a potential fall and automatically inflates airbags around the hips.

SafeStep integrates three technologies. AI-based motion prediction uses body telemetry from IMU, joint and pressure sensors to predict movement intentions and provide adaptive assistance. Robotic assistance with a safety clutch: BLDC motors and planetary gearboxes assist hip movement, while a torque-limiting clutch protects against excessive mechanical forces. Fall detection and airbag protection: it detects potential falls and automatically deploys airbags around the hips to reduce impact injuries.

Fall protection, step by step

Detect early. Deploy fast.

  1. 1≈ 0.1 s

    The algorithm recognises a change in body state.

  2. 2≈ 0.08 s

    Airbags inflate around the hips to protect the body.

  3. 3after

    Assist-to-stand helps the senior up; caregivers are alerted.

Reference timing from an existing airbag fall-protection system: the algorithm recognises the change in the person's state in about 0.1 s, and the airbags deploy within about 0.08 s to protect the body. In SafeStep, the assist-to-stand mode and caregiver alert follow.

Validation & testing

Project & my part

In our group project, we developed a smart exoskeleton with hip airbags to protect older people in Thailand from fall injuries and help them get up again after a fall. I was responsible for the Validation and Testing part of the pitch. I turned our product idea into five testable hypotheses (“We believe that…”) covering desirability, usability and feasibility. I then designed a three-phase validation plan and defined a metric for each hypothesis. I also contribted to the pitch slides together with my teammates.

What I learned

This part showed me how Ries’s Lean Startup approach works in practice: before building an expensive product, you have to identify the riskiest assumptions and test them as cheaply as possible. For us, that meant 15–20 interviews, a test with a demo video and a pre-registration page, and an energy calculation in Phase 1. In Phase 2 it meant an ESP32 + IMU box tested with public fall datasets, and a non-functional dummy belt worn by seniors. A full prototype with actuated joints and airbags only comes in Phase 3, and only if the earlier phases pass. Finally, I learned that the technical and the human sides cannot be separated. Detecting a fall early enough is a feasibility question, but whether seniors will wear the device for hours in Thailand’s heat and humidity is just as decisive.

Reflection 4 of 7, Léopold: turned the idea into five testable hypotheses and a three-phase validation plan with metrics. Lesson: test the riskiest assumptions cheaply first, and the technical and human sides belong together.
